PAPER: Domain-Agnostic Hardware Fingerprinting-Based Device Identifier for
       Zero-Trust IoT Security
AUTHORS: Abdurrahman Elmaghbub and Bechir Hamdaoui (Oregon State University)
SOURCE: IEEE Wireless Communications, April 2024 (Zero Trust Security Methods
         for Wireless Networks special section)

====================================================================
* ABSTRACT
====================================================================
Next-generation networks aim to seamlessly interconnect humans, machines,
devices, and systems, which raises privacy/security concerns since a
single compromise can affect the whole network. Zero Trust (ZT) is
positioned as the key paradigm to safeguard network integrity and data
confidentiality. The paper introduces EPS-CNN, a deep-learning-based
wireless device identification framework intended to serve as the
device-authentication layer of a ZT architecture, targeting
resource-constrained IoT devices. At its core, a CNN generates a device
identity from a novel RF signal representation — the Double-Sided
Envelope Power Spectrum (EPS) — which captures device-specific hardware
characteristics while discarding device-irrelevant information.
Experiments show testing accuracy of over 99% (same-domain: same day,
location, channel), 93% (cross-day), and 95% (cross-location). The
results demonstrate superior accuracy, robustness, and adaptability
compared to raw-IQ-based deep learning methods, offering a pioneering
solution for ZT-based IoT device identification.

====================================================================
* INTRODUCTION
====================================================================
- Motivation: massive IoT connectivity plus increasingly sophisticated
  cyberattacks have exposed the limits of traditional perimeter-based
  network security.
- Zero Trust (ZT) model: "never trust, always verify" — trust is never
  assumed, access is continuously verified based on device/user
  credentials regardless of network location; identity authentication is
  the most critical stage.
- ZT device-identity requirements: hardware root of trust, passwordless
  authentication (digital certificates with public/private keys),
  renewable/operational credentials, and a device registry for
  audit/access control — requiring cryptographic engines, security
  processors, and secure storage.
- Problem: these requirements are hard to meet for resource-constrained
  microcontroller-based IoT devices (size, power, cost limits);
  compromised "innocent" devices can cause data pollution, lateral
  movement, and DoS attacks.
- Proposed solution: EPS-CNN — a hardware device fingerprinting framework
  that builds a unique, unforgeable, hardware-based device identity
  without requiring expensive onboard security hardware. Device
  fingerprints arise from manufacturing imperfections in RF circuitry
  (e.g., oscillators) that create distinctive, physically-inseparable
  signal distortions.
- Deep Learning (DL) is highlighted as a powerful tool already used for
  device discovery, vulnerability analysis, anomaly detection, and
  trust-based policy recommendation in IoT/ZT contexts.
- Key limitation of prior DL fingerprinting work: reliance on raw IQ data
  as input, which contains a lot of device-irrelevant information
  (channel, location, time artifacts), causing poor generalization when
  test data comes from a different "domain" than training data
  ("needle in a haystack" problem).
- Core contribution: EPS, a novel RF representation that isolates the
  oscillator's impairment (a key, domain-resilient hardware fingerprint
  source), improving robustness against time/channel/location domain
  shifts.

Key contributions listed by the authors:
1. EPS — a novel IQ representation improving deep-learning device
   identifier performance and shown to be distinguishable/reliable across
   time, channel, and location domains.
2. EPS-CNN — an EPS-based fingerprinting framework substantially
   enhancing accuracy/robustness of device identification under domain
   variation.
3. Empirical assessment on real WiFi devices, achieving average
   cross-domain testing accuracy of 93% (cross-day) and 95%
   (cross-location).

====================================================================
* BACKGROUND
====================================================================
- Zero Trust security paradigm (BeyondCorp [1], NIST ZTA SP 800-207 [2])
  and "Seven Properties of Highly Secure Devices" [3] underpin the
  device-identity requirements discussed.
- RF/Hardware fingerprinting: a physical-layer security technique that
  extracts unique signatures from a device's transmitted RF signal,
  caused by inherent manufacturing imperfections in RF circuitry
  components.
- Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO): a hardware impairment from mismatch
  between the local oscillators of transmitter and receiver, caused by
  oscillator inaccuracy/instability, Doppler shifts, or synchronization
  errors — a well-known RF hardware fingerprinting factor [12,13].
- Prior DL-based fingerprinting (using raw IQ data) is known from
  literature ([6],[8]-[10]) to degrade sharply when tested under domains
  (channel, location, day) different from training — this is the central
  gap the paper addresses.

====================================================================
* OBJECTIVES
====================================================================
1. Investigate and empirically confirm that Carrier Frequency Offset
   (CFO), caused by local oscillator inaccuracy, is the root cause of the
   sinusoidal envelope behavior observed in IQ signals of IoT devices.
2. Design a novel RF signal representation (EPS) that captures this
   oscillator-driven fingerprint while suppressing device-irrelevant
   information, to be resilient to time, channel, and location domain
   shifts.
3. Build EPS-CNN, a CNN-based device identification framework using EPS
   as input, suitable for integration into a Zero Trust IoT architecture
   as the device-authentication layer.
4. Empirically validate EPS-CNN's accuracy and robustness in same-domain
   and cross-domain (cross-location, cross-day, random-location)
   scenarios using a real 15-device WiFi testbed, and compare against a
   baseline CNN using raw IQ input (IQ-CNN).

====================================================================
* METHODOLOGY
====================================================================
Two-part methodology: (A) an empirical/simulation study establishing the
physical root cause of device fingerprints, and (B) the design and
evaluation of the EPS-CNN identification framework.

A. Understanding Oscillator Frequency Inaccuracy's Impact on IQ Signals
   - Testbed: 15 Pycom/IoT devices (identical hardware) transmitting
     identical IEEE 802.11b WiFi packets, captured via a USRP B210
     receiver, after 12 minutes of warm-up (to ensure hardware
     stabilization/consistent measurement).
   - Observation: I and Q time-domain signal envelopes show a
     "sinusoidal" pattern with a device-specific number of "humps."
   - Hypothesis: Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO), due to oscillator
     inaccuracy, causes this pattern; the number of humps depends on CFO
     value, and CFO differs by device due to manufacturing variation.
   - Validation: MATLAB simulation of a full WLAN 802.11b system with
     controlled CFO values (0, 50, 100, 200 Hz) through an AWGN channel;
     confirmed hump count scales with CFO. Other impairments (IQ
     imbalance, phase noise, DC offset) tested and did NOT produce the
     sinusoidal envelope effect — isolating CFO/oscillator as the cause.

B. EPS Representation Generation Pipeline
   1. Take received IQ frame r(t).
   2. Apply an FIR Hilbert transform filter (Parks-McClellan algorithm)
      to build an analytic signal; scale by an imaginary unit factor and
      combine with a time-delayed original signal (delay compensates for
      FIR filter's inherent group delay, half the filter length).
   3. Compute the signal envelope e(t) as the absolute value of the
      analytic signal.
   4. Downsample envelope by factor of 15; apply a lowpass filter to
      reduce ringing and smooth the envelope.
   5. Center the envelope amplitude around zero.
   6. Compute the normalized double-sided power spectrum of the envelope
      -> this is the EPS representation.
   - EPS is generated separately for I and Q components -> EPS(I) and
     EPS(Q), later combined into a single tensor for the CNN.

C. EPS-CNN Framework Design (for Zero-Trust IoT integration)
   1. Enrollment/Registration: new IoT device's IQ frame r(t) (dimension
      1x25170) is captured; EPS generator computes EPS(I) and EPS(Q),
      combined into a 2x4096 tensor.
   2. CNN Architecture: six convolutional blocks (each = 2D-Convolution +
      BatchNormalization + LeakyReLU + MaxPooling) extract fingerprint
      features from the EPS tensor; followed by three fully-connected
      layers (with LeakyReLU activations) and a final Softmax layer that
      outputs the predicted device identity.
   3. Runtime operation: when a device tries to access the network, it
      first undergoes rogue device detection (confirm legitimate
      affiliation), then identity verification using the trained EPS-CNN
      model. Continuous authentication is performed at the packet level
      using incoming RF signals; any deviation from the enrolled EPS
      fingerprint triggers an alert for investigation.

D. Evaluation Methodology
   - Testbed: 15 Pycom devices + USRP B210 receiver (Fig. 5).
   - Domains tested: channel (wired vs. wireless), location (Loc A = 1m,
     Loc B = 2m, Loc C = 3m, Loc D = random within 3m), and time
     (3 consecutive days).
   - 5-fold cross-validation (each device's data split into 5
     non-overlapping equal partitions).
   - Baseline for comparison: IQ-CNN — identical CNN architecture but fed
     raw IQ data instead of EPS.
   - Metrics: same-domain accuracy (train/test same domain) and
     cross-domain accuracy (train/test different domain — location,
     channel, or day).

====================================================================
* METHODS
====================================================================
- Signal processing method: EPS generation via Hilbert-transform-based
  analytic signal construction, envelope extraction, downsampling,
  lowpass filtering, zero-centering, and power spectrum estimation
  (detailed above under Methodology B).
- Machine learning method: Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with 6
  convolutional blocks (Conv2D + BatchNorm + LeakyReLU + MaxPooling),
  3 fully-connected layers with LeakyReLU, and a Softmax output layer for
  multi-class device classification.
- Baseline comparison method: IQ-CNN — same CNN architecture, fed raw IQ
  data instead of EPS, used to isolate the benefit of the EPS
  representation itself.
- Experimental design: controlled variation of domain factors (channel:
  wired/wireless; location: 1m/2m/3m/random; time: 3 consecutive days)
  with 5-fold cross-validation for robust accuracy estimates.
- Statistical/physical validation method: MATLAB-based simulation of a
  full WLAN 802.11b transceiver chain to isolate CFO as the causal factor
  behind the sinusoidal envelope (control variable: CFO in Hz; controlled
  channel: AWGN).

====================================================================
* RESULTS
====================================================================
Root-cause validation:
- CFO simulation confirmed that the number of envelope "humps" scales
  with CFO value (0/50/100/200 Hz), validating CFO/oscillator inaccuracy
  as the fingerprint source; other impairments (IQ imbalance, phase
  noise, DC offset) did not reproduce this effect.

Same-domain accuracy:
- EPS-CNN and IQ-CNN both achieve very high accuracy when trained/tested
  on the same location, channel, or day (e.g., same-location average
  accuracy for EPS-CNN: 100% at Loc A, 99.6% at Loc B, 96.7% at Loc C;
  same-day accuracy 100% (EPS-CNN) vs 99% (IQ-CNN)).

Cross-location accuracy (fixed different locations):
- Training on Loc A / testing on Loc C: EPS-CNN accuracy >95%; IQ-CNN
  accuracy below 30%.
- Similar large gains for other train/test location combinations
  (Loc B, Loc C) — described as the highest reported cross-domain
  performance for DL-based device fingerprinting at the time.

Cross-location accuracy (random placement):
- Trained on Loc A (fixed 1m), tested on random locations within 3m:
  EPS-CNN average accuracy 93% and 98% for two random deployments;
  IQ-CNN dropped to only 40% and 58% respectively.

Cross-day (temporal) accuracy:
- EPS-CNN average testing accuracy: 93% (Day 2) and 92% (Day 3) when
  trained on Day 1, versus 88% and 89% respectively for IQ-CNN.
- Similar improvements when trained on Days 2/3 and tested on other days.

Channel robustness:
- EPS representation remained visually/quantitatively stable across
  wired vs. wireless channel conditions over 3 consecutive days, for all
  15 tested devices.

Additional security/privacy benefits noted:
- EPS-CNN reduces transmission of device credential information
  (privacy-preserving) and removes the need for manual, error-prone MAC
  Authentication Bypass/allow-list maintenance, since policy
  recommendations derive automatically from the model's output.
- Framework durability depends on hardware aging rate (esp. crystal
  oscillator quality); current evaluation covers only short-term aging
  (~1 month), a standard practice for new fingerprinting techniques.

====================================================================
* CONCLUSION
====================================================================
The paper addresses the security challenge posed by resource-constrained,
microcontroller-based IoT devices that lack the capability for robust,
identity-based access authentication required by Zero Trust
architectures. EPS-CNN is presented as an innovative wireless device
identification solution tailored specifically for ZT IoT integration. Its
core innovation, the EPS representation, isolates the oscillator-driven
hardware fingerprint while discarding domain-irrelevant information,
overcoming a major limitation (poor cross-domain generalization) of
prior raw-IQ-based deep learning fingerprinting methods. Rigorous
empirical validation — spanning same-domain and cross-domain (location,
random-location, and time) scenarios on a real 15-device WiFi testbed —
demonstrates the practical viability of the approach, positioning it as
a meaningful contribution toward secure, ZT-compliant IoT networks in an
era of escalating cyber threats.
